How I work

Building with agents, as a system.

Getting an AI agent to produce something is easy and mostly worthless. The work is in the structure around it — what it's allowed to claim, what checks it, and what happens when it's confidently wrong. Here's how I've built that across next4 and Hirely.

01 — Distribution

An agent-run marketing org

next4's marketing has no team. A structured datastore holds seven content pillars, six channels, and the weekly schedule; agents run a Monday research scrape and email me each day's posts with full scripts and the assets to prepare. It produces 14 posts a week, and I work from the email, never from a dashboard.

02 — Guardrails

Rules that can kill a post

Twelve non-negotiables sit between a draft and a channel: never a chance percentage, never an unsourced school statistic, no identifying information about minors, verify every deadline against the official source. A finished "ChatGPT hallucinated this" video was cut after verification proved it hadn't — the replacement was both true and a better argument.

03 — Engineering

Spec, plan, build, adversarial review

I build through Claude Code in a fixed loop: written spec, task plan, subagent execution with the test written first, then a separate review pass whose only job is to find what the builder missed. It has caught a scoring measure that was quietly tracking family wealth, and a bug that passed its own unit test.
